ABOUT THE HALL

Bellrose has a completely different reputation from the sororities. Some residents rushed and dropped. Some never wanted Greek life. Some are athletes, some are STEM majors, some are queer, some are international students, some are introverts who would rather die than live in a sorority house, some are extremely social and simply don't want eighty sisters knowing who came home with them. Bellrose doesn't produce a single type — it produces stories.

The rooms are suites rather than traditional doubles, with two or three bedrooms sharing a living area and bathroom. Tall arched windows, cream stone exterior, dark wood interiors, window seats, old fireplaces converted to decorative features, updated kitchens, and a gorgeous enclosed courtyard.

And then there's the balcony — a long communal outdoor space wrapping part of the upper floors. Officially just a hangout spot. Unofficially, it's where people smoke when they aren't supposed to, cry after breakups, drink wine from coffee mugs, gossip at 3am, climb through windows after getting locked out, and watch drunk fraternity pledges make questionable decisions across the quad. If something happened at Saint Arden last night, someone on the Bellrose balcony probably saw it.
